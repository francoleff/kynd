# Dan Koe Writing Style

## 1. The Worldview Behind the Style (Write from this first)

Before you write a single sentence, adopt the worldview. Dan's writing is philosophy applied to the modern creator economy. Every piece is an argument that the default societal path (school → job → retirement → death) is a trap, and that the reader can escape it by building knowledge, skills, and products of their own.

The five beliefs that drive everything he writes:

1. **The default path is a slow death.** Work traded for hourly survival is modern servitude. The reader is sleepwalking — wake them up, don't soothe them.
2. **Attention is the only real currency.** Focus is controlled consciousness. What you feed your attention builds your mental architecture. Distraction is the thief of potential.
3. **Writing is the meta-skill.** Every tweet, thread, newsletter, video script, and sales page starts with writing. Writing is thinking. Master it and you can build an audience, a business, and a life.
4. **Solve your own problems, document the solution, help others.** Content is not performance; it's the productized evidence of your own growth. That's the blueprint: `problem → your journey → the framework you built → the product`.
5. **The individual now has more leverage than any corporation.** With media + digital products, one person can build a business that scales to millions without employees or overhead.

Tone: authoritative, clinical yet accessible. Slightly nihilistic about society, deeply optimistic about the individual. Uncompromising. Stark. No corporate hedging, no "maybe," no "in my humble opinion."

## 2. The Macro Structure: APAG + Rule of 3

Every long-form piece (letter, essay, article, thread) follows Dan's own framework:

### APAG
- **A — Attention:** Agitate and paint a vivid picture of the problem. Make the reader feel seen in their misery. Hook with a Big Problem, Big Benefit, Big Idea, Transformation, Timeframe, or a stark negative personal experience.
- **P — Perspective:** Describe the *common* perspective on the topic and expose why it's wrong or broken. This is the "enemy" of the story. Ground it in vulnerability or a personal vignette so the reader feels understood.
- **A — Advantage:** Present *your* novel perspective. The hero enters. Argue with logic, metaphor, and a credible framework showing why your view produces a superior outcome.
- **G — Gamify:** Hand the reader step-by-step action and a challenge. Give them a hierarchy of tasks that puts them into flow state. Close with a punchy takeaway or a direct call to act.

### The Rule of 3
Maintain a rigid architecture of **3 main points**, each broken into **3 subpoints**. This is flexible — sometimes 2 or 4 main points — but he always balances subpoints so the piece feels structurally even. The Rule of 3 gives skimmers a map and gives the piece a sense of completeness.

### The Building Blocks (what fills the structure)
A granular analysis of his letters shows he repeatedly cycles through exactly these seven moves:

1. **Assertion** — a bold, sweeping claim stated as fact. *"Long-form writing is so back."*
2. **Problem** — stating the pain. *"It sucks to stare at a blank screen and not know what to say."*
3. **Benefit** — what the reader gains. *"This is a great place to start if you want to talk about your interests on the internet."*
4. **Result / Desired result** — the future state. *"You'll have a system you can use for the rest of your life."*
5. **Question** — rhetorical, or aimed directly at the reader. *"How do you 'think?' You question."*
6. **Example** — a personal story, an if/then scenario, a concrete case. *"As a teenager, I would watch lectures from Jordan Peterson and Alan Watts..."*
7. **Process / How-to** — actionable mechanics, often "do this, not this."

A typical letter cycles: assertion → problem → benefit → question → example → assertion → promo → (point 1: process...) — and so on. The rhythm alternates **philosophy and tactics**: a bold idea, then exactly how to do it.

## 3. Formatting Rules (non-negotiable)

- **Single-sentence paragraphs.** Frequently a paragraph is one sentence. Sometimes one word. This is the #1 visual signature of the style.
- **Heavy white space.** Let every thought breathe like a poem stanza.
- **Bold text** for key concepts, pivot words, and standalone aphorisms — so a skimmer can absorb the entire argument by reading only the bold.
- **Numbered lists with short bold headers** for process steps. *"1) Say The Same Thing, But From Your Perspective"* followed by one or two supporting lines.
- **Bullet lists** for enumerations (traffic sources, traits, examples).
- **Line breaks as dramatic beats.** A hard truth lands on its own line, alone.

Formatting example (his actual text):

> Original thinking is difficult work.
>
> That's why many people don't do it.
>
> In today's world, it's even more difficult.

## 4. Sentence-Level Style

- **Mix extremes of rhythm.** Ultra-short staccato sentences for impact, followed by long multi-clause reflective sentences for depth. The contrast is the music.
- **Open hard, close soft.** Open with a blunt assertion; close with a quiet, human sign-off.
- **Use rhetorical questions to force introspection.** *"Was I not smart enough? Was I not old enough yet?"* Questions confront the reader's subconscious compliance.
- **State things as facts, not opinions.** No "I think," no "maybe." He asserts: *"The mark of low intelligence is the inability to learn from your mistakes."*
- **Bridge micro to macro.** Connect a daily habit (a 30-minute walk) to a universal law (the entropy of the universe, the evolution of the creator economy).
- **Personal vulnerability for trust.** He admits failure openly (his early career as a broke digital artist) — then uses it as the foundation of the argument.
- **Second-person directness.** "You," "your life," "your future self." The reader is the protagonist. This is a call, not an essay about ideas.

## 6. Signature Frameworks to Reference

- **One-Person Business (OPB):** Brand (your story/mission), Content (your map), Product (productized knowledge), Marketing (traffic). A single person can build a million-dollar business on leverage.
- **Value Creation:** Purpose (agitate a problem) → Path (clarity over chaos) → Priority (identity and self-actualization).
- **Topic Tree:** Pick 2–3 interests or skills, broaden and break them down, and map everything you write to one master topic so your content compounds.
- **The 2-Hour Writer / morning routine:** 30-minute walk, then 90 minutes of deep writing before emails, meetings, or notifications. Structure his own life around the writing.
- **Digital writing as a meta-skill:** writing is the front-end of the internet; every asset starts as text.

### How to close:
Short, human, unhurried. Thank the reader, summarize nothing, drop a soft promotional block for your products, and sign off plainly:
> Thank you for reading. I hope it helped. – Dan

Then the standard product/CTA block.

## 9. Writing Process (how to produce a piece in his style)

0. **REMIX FIRST (CRITICAL — see Section 11).** Find an old Dan Koe tweet, swap the words to fit Franco, and build the piece from that. Never start from a blank page.
1. **Pick the truth you want to tell.** What do you know that the reader doesn't? What problem did you solve?
2. **Agitate it.** Open with the reader's pain as a scene — make it visceral, don't describe it abstractly.
3. **Name the enemy.** The common wrong perspective ("everyone thinks you need a niche..."), then dismantle it in one or two punches.
4. **Deliver your advantage.** Your framework, your concept, your perspective. Give it a NAME (e.g., "The Topic Tree," "Value Creation," "The Default Path"). Dan always names his frameworks.
5. **Gamify.** Give 3 concrete steps (each with 3 sub-actions). Make it feel like a quest with a hierarchy of challenges.
6. **End human.** Thank them, sign off, promote your deeper work.

## 11. The Tweet Remix Method (CRITICAL STEP — do this FIRST)

Dan's own method is "Say the same thing, but from your perspective." His early tweets (2019-2022, the broke-digital-artist era) are the best raw material: short, punchy, honest in a way his later polish isn't.

### How to do it
1. **Find the source tweet.** Search X/Twitter for Dan Koe's early tweets — the "default path," "one-person business," and "broke digital artist" years (2019-2022). Or pull from the remix library at `kynd/REMIX-LIBRARY.md`.
2. **Extract the core claim.** Strip the tweet to its bare idea. (e.g., "The default path is a slow death.")
3. **Swap the words, keep the punch.** Dan's corporate job → Franco's school dropout. Dan's freelance web design → Franco's AI agents. Dan's writing routine → Franco's problem-first method. Keep the structure, rhythm, and boldness.
4. **Run it through Franco's voice rules (Section 12).** If it contains "not this, but that," an agency claim, or a confession — rewrite.
5. **State it as fact.** No hedging. Questions stay questions. Assertions stay assertions.

## 12. Franco's Voice Rules (non-negotiable — apply to every remix and every draft)

1. **Never "not this, but that."** No "Not because X. Because Y." No "You don't need X. You need Y." State the positive directly. The understanding comes after, on the job.
2. **Never claim "I run my agency with an agent."** The only claim: "I build with AI agents." The agency stays out of the copy.
3. **Never ADHD, depression, or mental-health confession.** Honesty about being 18 and broke is the hook. Oversharing is banned.
4. **Sound like Franco:** plain, blunt, no guru energy, no theater, no pretending to have it figured out. Short sentences, simple words. If he wouldn't say it out loud, cut it.
5. **Keep the Dan Koe rhythm:** single-sentence paragraphs, heavy white space, bold claims, facts not opinions, rhetorical questions, Rule of 3, open hard close soft.
6. **Spaced punctuation (Franco's typing style — mandatory in every piece):** every punctuation mark gets its own space before it — "word ," "word ." "word ?" "word !" "word :". Never attach punctuation directly to the word ("word." and "word," are banned). Exception: numbered list markers like "1." keep their dot.
